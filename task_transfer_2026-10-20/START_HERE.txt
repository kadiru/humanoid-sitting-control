What we have actually been doing

1. Physical simulation experiments and model corrections.

   Early work tested landing timing, balance, arm counterbalance, feedback, measured-geometry coverage, and guarded contact transfer. Some experiments improved the baseline; many failed. We also discovered that certain model screens were incomplete: a tilted endpoint could violate the resting definition, the cooked collision surface differed from the raw planning mesh, and being motionless while hovering did not count as sitting.

   The most recent physical candidate recorded in the current status is experiment 273. Its selected cases could hover without establishing legal seat load or completing the required quiet release. It was rejected. It did not replace best09.

2. Offline construction of an entire admissible sitting motion.

   Work shifted toward finding a complete motion that simultaneously obeys foot placement, balance, housing clearance, joint limits, actuator command rates, filter behavior, contact transfer, timing, and finite-precision execution rules. These calculations run on saved model data; they are different from running the robot in simulation.

   Some offline paths satisfied geometry and static checks but failed command rates, target bounds, timing, or finite execution checks. For example, the latest new nonlinear-model construction, 484, improved several constraints but still failed the original command-rate and target constraints. It was not accepted as an executable repair.

3. Large linear feasibility problems and attempts to simplify them.

   To construct a valid next motion update, we built a linear program: a large collection of linear inequalities and equalities representing the saved motion and its restrictions. A “witness” means a complete vector that independently passes those constraints. It is not yet a successful physical motion.

   The original representation had 78,791 variables. We tried exact removal of duplicate or implied rows, alternative optimization formulations, and algebraic elimination of variables determined by mechanical equalities. Each substantive change required a separate proof/audit so that fewer equations would not silently remove physical requirements.

   Several bounded solver attempts finished without an admissible witness. Two other algorithm families also stopped without one. A partial solution in experiment 512 failed omitted constraints and is explicitly unusable. Solver timeouts do not prove that the original problem is impossible.

4. The most recent numerical solver attempt: experiment 598.

   Exact algebra reduced the optimizer to 10,541 coordinates, with other coordinates reconstructed through saved equations. However, the resulting inequalities became much denser: about 6.10 million nonzero coefficients. Fewer variables did not demonstrate a faster or better problem.

   A single cold HiGHS interior-point solve was attempted under a 45-second total allowance, including preparation. It reported six interior-point iterations, exceeded the charged allowance by about 0.68 seconds, and returned NO candidate vector. The charged total was 45.683 seconds; only about 26.878 seconds remained as the backend allowance after preparation.

   Therefore no reconstruction, new model evaluation, new physical trial, or PPO update followed. Audit 599 verified the accounting and saved result. Report 600 and closure record 601 document that failure to obtain a witness; “closure” means closing that experiment's records, not completing the user task.

5. The most recent scientific experiment: 602, independently audited in 603.

   We tested whether one local pressure degree of freedom could be removed while preserving every affected modeled output. An exact calculation showed that it could not, for the specific saved block being tested. This is a useful negative result about that proposed simplification, not a proof that sitting or the full optimization problem is infeasible. No new reduced problem or solver was launched.
