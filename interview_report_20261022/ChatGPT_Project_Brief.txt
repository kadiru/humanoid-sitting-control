HUMANOID SITTING CONTROL RESEARCH REPORT

Project evidence and interview preparation for Kadir
Snapshot: October 22, 2026 Pacific time / October 23, 2026 UTC

We are developing and evaluating a humanoid sitting controller in simulation, with the current work focused on low seats that slope toward the feet. The aim is to establish stable, legal seat contact without slipping or unwanted robot-housing contact, preserve all existing successes, and then compare a repaired controller with new reinforcement-learning policies, measuring success before actuator effort.

The project has a functioning baseline, a measured-sensor control pipeline, and a detailed experimental record. The remaining repair is unfinished: the best controller passes 64 of 72 development trials, and none of the eight remaining failures has been repaired. Recent numerical progress has not yet passed the checks required to execute a new motion. [S1, S2]

What completion means

The required outcome is a repaired controller verified across all 72 complete development trials, followed by the specified new PPO comparison and reserved evaluation, plus an updated video covering the attempts in less than one minute. Passing the development bank would establish success on that bank; it would not establish general sitting ability or real-robot deployment.

The eight failures are four surface conditions in two placements each: 36 cm / +15°, 36 cm / +20°, 37 cm / +16.25°, and 36.5 cm / +17.5°. The 88.9% figure is a test pass rate, not a percentage of project completion. [S2]

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND TOOLS

The simulation uses the G1 humanoid asset in Isaac Lab. The approach combines geometric motion planning, measured feedback, guarded contact transfer, and previously learned policy components. Earlier PPO work learned bounded corrections around a frozen motion scaffold; it was not learning sitting entirely from scratch. [S7]

Runtime flow: measured depth and proprioception → seat geometry and state estimates → motion reference, feedback and learned corrections → guards and filtered joint commands → simulated actuators and contact. Offline optimization proposes future motion updates; it does not directly replace this guarded pipeline.

PPO means proximal policy optimization; IMU means inertial measurement unit; PD means proportional–derivative joint control. Proprioception refers here to joint and body-motion measurements.

Runtime decisions use measured camera depth, encoder, IMU and torque-equivalent signals, together with a static robot model and reference. Privileged simulator information is restricted to reset, reward and evaluation. This information boundary is central to the experiment, although hardware transfer remains untested. [S2, S7]

PERCEPTION AND MODELING FINDINGS

Recovering seat measurements when the rear view is lost

The rear camera can lose a usable seat-plane fit during the motion. A recorded diagnostic checked the already defined downward camera as an additional view. It produced valid fits for all 132 targeted frames in which the rear tracker was unavailable; the largest recorded height and slope errors there were 1.565 mm and 0.132°. These are results for that diagnostic, not universal perception accuracy. [S3]

[Figure] Figure 1. Actual recorded simulated depth at 12 seconds from diagnostic 39. The rear view has no fit in these examples; the downward view does. This recording itself did not control the robot. A subsequent revision introduced a guarded current-frame fallback. [S3]

The runtime change retained the rear view as preferred and allowed the current downward fit only for selected cases when the rear fit was unavailable. Plane conditioning, self masking, floor exclusion and patch-association rules stayed in place. This addressed a specific observation gap without substituting simulator geometry for measured input.

Finding a mismatch between planning and collision geometry

PhysX convex cooking changed the pelvis collision support surface by about 5–6 mm in recorded contact directions. A path checked against the raw mesh could therefore misestimate clearance. The project introduced a separate offline cooked collision model for planning while retaining the original geometry for perception self masking. This improved model fidelity; it did not certify a successful contact transition. [S3]

Aligning the planned endpoint with the actual objective

Some earlier paths targeted a 0.9 radian trunk tilt, about 51.6°, even though the resting criterion required at most 40°. The earlier model screens were incomplete. Subsequent planning used the unchanged 40° requirement with a separate 2° research reserve. The correction illustrates why the acceptance contract must be checked before optimizing a trajectory. [S3]

PHYSICAL EXPERIMENTS AND WHAT THEY TAUGHT US

These are schematic renderings from recorded simulation transforms, not camera photographs or a matched causal comparison. [S8]

A smoother model did not guarantee a successful landing

A force-allocation diagnostic found a 0.0795 radian command jump for only 0.00087 radians of pose change. A revised allocation removed that initial jump and reduced adjacent modeled ankle-torque reversals from roughly 26–27 Nm to below 0.5 Nm. The later physical probe still failed. Improving one numerical artifact was useful, but balance, tracking and contact transfer remained coupled problems. [S3]

Hovering without slipping is not sitting

The most recent physical candidate recorded in the handoff, experiment 273, could hover without establishing the required legal seat load and quiet release. It was rejected. A clean outcome requires complete-trial slip and contact checks, stable support and the resting definition, including trunk tilt at most 40° and legal seat normal load of at least 5% of body weight. [S2]

The historical 58.4-second video covers 864 complete trials across multiple experiments, 192 recorded prefixes, and 120 aborted diagnostic cases disclosed without invented motion. Those totals are an experiment archive, not additional independent successes for the current baseline. The video has not been updated with a repaired controller or the planned new PPO comparison. RECENT OPTIMIZATION PROGRESS AND FAILURE

Work shifted toward constructing a complete motion that satisfies foot pose, balance, housing clearance, joint bounds, command filtering and rates, contact transfer and timing together. One full linear representation has 78,791 variables and 652,529 inequalities. A candidate vector must pass the original constraints independently, then survive finite execution and nonlinear model checks before any simulated motion. [S1, S4]

Saved nonlinear model result | Value
Mismatch merit before update | 0.1979
Mismatch merit after update | 0.1227
Relative reduction | 38.0%
Actual motion admission | Failed

Mismatch merit is a normalized model measure. Its reduction is not a physical success-rate improvement, energy saving or controller-performance gain. [S4]

The update passed finite encoding and its independent audit. Fresh model and derivative evaluations covered 1,365 states in 8.27 seconds total. It was accepted as a research iterate, but actual numerical motion admission failed: residual mismatch included about 7.75 mm in foot position, 0.0266 rad in commands and 2.63 Nm in torque. No repaired motion followed. [S4]

Why reducing the problem size was insufficient

An earlier exact elimination reduced the optimizer to 10,541 coordinates but made its inequalities much denser, with about 6.10 million nonzeros. A cold HiGHS solve returned no candidate after six interior-point iterations and a charged total of 45.683 seconds, exceeding the 45-second allowance. Preparation and validation costs matter alongside solver time; fewer variables alone did not establish a speedup. [S2]

Outcome of the latest approved research cycle

Stage A identified the old correction’s obstruction: a right-ankle-roll planning lower bound at 13.14 seconds, with only about 2.50 × 10⁻¹⁰ rad of guarded margin. Stage B held that ankle coordinate fixed and released the existing pelvis-orientation freedom, but its predicted improvement was only 4.46 × 10⁻¹³ and failed the numerical progress floor. The cycle stopped there; later stages, simulation and PPO did not start. The audit verified the failure accounting, not a feasible candidate. [S5]

This is a negative result for that construction. It does not prove that the full motion problem or low-seat sitting is impossible. The physical score remains 64/72.

REMAINING WORK AND THE NEXT DECISION

The next milestone is one complete, admissible motion update that can advance to controlled simulation. There is currently no selected, launch-ready method. The latest local design note considers solving small state-local blocks inside the Newton system while retaining temporal coupling. It may reduce the size of the remaining global system, but the existing sparse solver may already exploit the same structure. Net speedup, numerical conditioning, initialization and complete resource costs are unresolved. [S6]

A new method must fit the inherited 45-second total scientific limit, including validation and audit, and the 40 GiB per-worker cap. The completed one-time diagnostic exceptions are consumed. They do not authorize retries, relaxed tolerances, reuse of rejected directions, or automatic downstream runs. No new experiment was launched to prepare this report.

How the final comparison will be measured

After the repair passes, the plan specifies two PPO seeds, 63111 and 63117, with 24 environments and eight complete-episode updates per seed. Each run has 192,000 active plus 4,200 indexed warmup transitions, or 196,200 per seed and 392,400 total; initial settling is accounted separately. The main runs start from the smoke test’s initial weights. [S2]

Report clean support, stability, slip, unwanted contact and lost or gained successes before effort. Compare effort only on common complete clean trials, across the full 20 seconds and final 10- and 5-second windows. Applied PD torque is a simulation effort proxy, not measured electrical energy. The fresh bank contains four unseen seat heights crossed with three positive slopes in two placements. [S2]

INTERVIEW DISCUSSION AND LESSONS

A concise project description

“This project investigates reliable humanoid sitting using a G1 simulation, measured depth and proprioceptive inputs, geometric control and learned policy components. The current baseline succeeds cleanly in 64 of 72 complete development trials. The difficult remaining cases are low, positively sloped seats where balance, clearance and actuator limits interact. The work exposed concrete model errors, including a 5–6 mm collision-geometry discrepancy and a planned trunk angle that violated the resting objective. More recent offline work reduced nonlinear model mismatch by 38%, but it still failed motion-admission checks. The next objective is a validated repair that preserves all existing successes, followed by a frozen two-seed PPO comparison and reserved evaluation.”

Technical topics worth preparing

- Data boundaries: explain why the controller uses measured inputs while simulation truth is allowed for evaluation and reward; describe how leakage would invalidate the claim.
- Debugging across layers: trace a failure from a camera or collision model through planning, actuator filtering, tracking and contact. Use one recorded example rather than listing every experiment.
- Numerical engineering: explain why a smaller matrix can be slower, what an independently checked feasible vector means, and why model improvement does not imply safe motion.
- Experiment design: explain complete-trial metrics, overlapping failure categories, regression retention, frozen budgets and why the fresh evaluation set remains unopened.
- Failure and prioritization: discuss when to stop a formulation, what evidence a new approach must add, and how to measure progress toward a useful controller.

What the setbacks say about the process

There is a real risk of overinvesting in numerical certification while physical performance stays flat. A useful lesson is to distinguish essential physical and regression criteria from mathematical ambitions such as finding an optimum. Limited feasibility-only diagnostics were explicitly tried; the latest still failed. The next approach needs a credible path to a usable update, not another unchanged design note. The eight remaining physical failures are the primary progress measure.

Earlier PPO experience reinforces this point. In a separate reward study, two 128-update runs produced 44/64 and 42/64 successes versus 47/64 for the starting policy; neither was promoted. This is a different benchmark from the current 72-case bank. More training and a changed reward did not automatically improve the evaluated behavior. [S9]
