Saved-data diagnosis: recovery-imitation PPO seed 64111, 256 updates
29 October 2026 UTC / 28 October PDT

Conclusion
The deployed imitation policy is unstable over a complete movement, and the
optimizer scarcely changes it. More unchanged PPO updates are not the next fix.
This is evidence of a failed imitation/optimization setup; it does not establish
a mathematical local optimum or impossibility under the current action limits.
No active training code, checkpoints, constraints, or GPU jobs were modified.

Executed evidence
1. Policy changes occurred in only 10 of 256 updates: 34 accepted actor steps
   versus 1,024 critic steps. Updates 64 and 128 have bit-identical actor weights.
   The last actor change was update 146. There were 246 updates without a change.
   All 251 ultimately rejected proposals had their last tested KL below 0.02.
   The log field rejected_kl_steps combines KL, objective, and imitation-anchor
   rejection, so calling these KL failures would be incorrect. Final anchor MSE
   was 0.0004575736 against a 0.0004577360 ceiling: only 0.0355% headroom. This
   strongly implicates the anchor/objective acceptance logic; exact rejection
   counts by cause are unavailable because rejected candidate anchor and full
   objective values were not saved. We did not replay optimization to invent them.

2. Full-bank, CPU-inferred imitation MSE increased from 0.0003782083 to
   0.0004697954. Desired-joint RMSE was 0.0005834 rad initially and 0.0006502 rad
   finally (about 0.037 degrees finally). Those are errors on familiar saved
   observations, not errors during autonomous control. In the final autonomous
   evaluation, the 64 protected cases were still alive at 4 seconds but differed
   from their reference by 0.09375 rad (5.37 degrees) joint-pose RMSE. Initial
   pose disagreement after the first step was only 0.00001075 rad. The evidence
   supports accumulating control/observation-distribution error, not a gross
   initial reset displacement. It is not a controlled causal experiment.

3. Recovery round 0 used the teacher for 98.2% of control ticks, allowing at
   most 0.02 seconds of uninterrupted policy control. Round 1 used the teacher
   for 91%, allowing at most 0.10 seconds. These successful assisted trials
   therefore do not demonstrate recovery after seconds of autonomous drift.
   Source review also shows that recovery targets are the reference commands at
   the recorded time, not a feedback expert recomputing a stabilizing action
   from the deviated state. Only clean assisted trajectories enter the fit.

4. Final full-standing evaluation: 0 clean, 0 stable, 72 physical failures,
   median first failure 4.52 seconds (range 4.08–10.30). At first failure,
   69 had an impact violation and 33 forbidden contacts; categories overlap.
   Impact measurements were 2.59–10.68 bodyweights, median 5.77, against the
   unchanged 2-bodyweight limit. Forbidden contacts included 31 outside-patch
   cases and 2 over-10N wrong-contact cases. No first failure was attributed to
   a fall, torque violation, or joint margin. Later post-failure flags are not
   used to assign the original cause. No evaluation sample achieved a good hold.

5. Mean full-evaluation reward was -57.28 for the clone, -29.69 at update 128,
   and -36.67 at 256. Yet median first failure worsened from 8.60 to 6.42 to
   4.52 seconds, and qualifying hold samples fell from 1,783 to zero. Thus a
   less-negative score does not demonstrate progress. In the final evaluation,
   mean tracking reward was +9.68; contact, settling, hold, and effort rewards
   were zero; wrong-contact cost was -21.78 and terminal cost -20.00. Offline
   reward reconstruction matches saved rewards within 6.54e-5 across all three
   evaluations (float arithmetic). Reward differences are descriptive, not a
   proof the policy intentionally learned to terminate early.

6. Curriculum: the 648 completed training episodes included 288 standing-start
   attempts, all 0 clean and 0 stable; 104 starts at 6s also had zero successes.
   The only clean training episodes were 1 from a 10s reset and 11 from a 15s
   reset. Those begin partway through the movement and are not complete sitting
   successes. Fixed update-count advancement therefore moved into harder stages
   without evidence the prior stages were mastered. Another 13 all-dead batches
   ended early and were correctly recorded as failures, not successes.

7. Successful original demonstrations obey the same action bounds (maximum
   command increment 0.003000021 rad, the already-approved float32 rounding).
   During the first 4 seconds, the rate cap was active for 9.22% of protected
   teacher commands versus 8.54% for the final policy. This does not prove the
   eight original failures are repairable under the limits, but does rule out
   the limits alone explaining loss of all 64 demonstrated successes.
   Saved sensor audits show valid observations before failure, median surface
   offset error about 2 mm, and median normal error about 0.12 degrees. These
   audits do not certify newly introduced furniture or all perception behavior.

What needs fixing after the already-running comparison
A. Make imitation work through a whole movement. Obtain state-corrective expert
   targets at states actually reached by the learner, including pre-failure
   states; aggregate those labels instead of keeping only near-teacher clean
   trajectories. Gradually increase independent control beyond 0.10 seconds.
   Check multi-step drift and complete 20s starts, not just average action MSE.
   The present time-indexed replay is not a substitute for a recovery expert.
B. Replace the current acceptance deadlock in a separately versioned experiment.
   Log each rejection predicate and candidate value first. Design policy steps
   that satisfy the imitation constraint while improving the objective (or a
   justified soft imitation objective), rather than repeatedly rejecting the
   same class of proposals. Keep physical criteria and actuator limits intact;
   do not silently loosen any constraint or modify the running experiment.
C. Make curriculum advancement depend on measured autonomous success. Preserve
   standing-start exposure and distinguish assisted/partial-start successes from
   complete sitting. The first objective is restoration of demonstrated behavior;
   repair claims still require all 72 original trials and retention of the 64.
D. Validate task-reward ranking on these saved complete trajectories. Earlier
   hard failures should not look like better sitting; provide useful approach,
   deceleration, and safe-support progress signals before successful contact,
   while retaining original hard termination and final physical success tests.
   Evaluate reward components by matched reset stage, not across curriculum mixes.

Priority: correct closed-loop imitation and the near-frozen actor update path
before another unchanged PPO run. Tuning reward weights alone cannot fix this.
The exact benefit of a proposed change requires a new validated experiment;
no repair, counterfactual success, or furniture correctness is claimed here.
